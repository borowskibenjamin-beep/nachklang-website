About the name

Why "Nachklang"?

The word

Nachklang (n.), German — pronounced NAHKH-klahng

Literally: the sound that remains after a tone has stopped ringing. The reverberation. What lingers in the air after the note itself is gone.

There's no exact English equivalent. The closest words — echo, reverberation, resonance — each capture only part of it: an echo implies a repeat, a reverberation is purely physical, resonance is too vague. Nachklang is specifically about what's left behind after something has ended.

Why we chose it

After a death, what remains isn't the person — it's everything they leave behind: contracts, obligations, accounts that keep charging, memories that still need tending. We chose "Nachklang" because it holds two things at once: dignity (not a clinical name like "Estate Manager" or "Subscription Canceller") and precision (it describes exactly the phase we help with — what's left to do after the loss itself).

One name, everywhere

Nachklang started in Germany, Austria, and Switzerland. As we bring it to more countries, the name stays the same everywhere — in every language, on every store listing. One name, one promise: that this part, at least, can be handled with care, wherever you are.
